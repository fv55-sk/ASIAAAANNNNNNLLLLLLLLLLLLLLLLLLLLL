Slide-by-Slide Review

1. Regionalism vs. Globalization (Slide 3 & 7)

REGIONALISM: Formal intergovernmental collaboration between two or more states in a geographic region. Expresses shared identity, purpose, and regional institutions.
GLOBALIZATION (Manfred Steger): Expansion and intensification of social relations worldwide. 4 Claims: Market liberalization, inevitable & irreversible, nobody in charge, benefits everyone long-term.

2. The 3 Globalization Framework Lenses (Slide 6)

1. Externalist View: Asia as an object impacted by Western forces (colonial rule, Bretton Woods IMF/WB, 1997 Crisis, McWorld).
2. Generative View: Asia as a springboard/driver of globalization (spice trade, Chinese production, Indian IT, OFWs remittance = 11% Ph GDP, K-Pop/Squid Game).
3. Alternative to Globalization: Anti-global impulse ("Asian Way" - consensus/harmony over individual freedom, East Asian Co-Prosperity Sphere, AMF, Santi Suk currency).

The 4 Waves of Middle Class Formation (Slides 24-46)

Key Regional Engine
1ST WAVE (1950s-70s) Post-WWII

The Rise of Japan

Post-war US support & export manufacturing (Toyota, Sony, Panasonic). Model of export-led growth.

2ND WAVE (1970s-90s) 4 Asian Tigers

South Korea, Taiwan, HK, Singapore

Semiconductors (Taiwan), Finance (HK/Singapore), Tech/Auto (Samsung/Hyundai). Rapid export expansion.

3RD WAVE (1980s-90s) SEA Tigers

Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Ph

BPO & OFWs (Philippines), Palm oil/petroleum (Malaysia), Autos (Thailand). Faced political instability & corruption challenges.

4TH WAVE (1978+) Urban China

Deng Xiaoping Reforms

Special Economic Zones (SEZs), foreign investment, tech & e-commerce (Shanghai, Shenzhen). Lifted 100s of millions.

3 Driving Forces of Regionalism (Slides 55-59)

  • 1. Management of Independence: Former colonies creating mechanisms to administer relations peacefully.
  • 2. Management of Interdependence: Setting regional rules for security, trade, and investment flows.
  • 3. Management of Internationalization: Using regional blocs as "building blocks" for global trade.

Integration Approaches (Slide 54)

SUPRANATIONALISM: States pool sovereignty, surrendering individual veto power to central regional authorities (e.g., European Union).
INTERGOVERNMENTALISM: Integration strictly based on non-interference/non-intervention in domestic affairs (e.g., ASEAN / "The ASEAN Way").

ASEAN Architecture (Slides 63-67)

  • Founded: Aug 8, 1967 (Bangkok Declaration).
  • 5 Founding Fathers: Adam Malik (Indonesia), Narciso Ramos (Ph), Tun Abdul Razak (Malaysia), S. Rajaratnam (Singapore), Thanat Khoman (Thailand).
  • 3 Pillars (Vision 2015): Security Community, Economic Community, Socio-Cultural Community.
